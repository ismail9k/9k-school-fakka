# Fakka: product brief

What Fakka is, who it is for, and what its launch page must do. When a request disagrees with this brief, ask before acting.

## The product

**Fakka (فكّة)** is an upcoming app that helps people manage their personal expenses. The name is Egyptian Arabic for "small change": the coins that disappear without anyone noticing, which is exactly the problem Fakka solves.

- **The problem:** at the end of the month, most people don't know where their salary went.
- **The promise:** record an expense in seconds, and see each month how much you spent and on what.
- **Who it is for:** people in Egypt, roughly 20 to 35 years old, who earn a monthly salary or freelance income, and who manage their money on their phone.
- **Status:** the app is not built yet. Its features, price, and launch date are not final.

## The launch page

Before the app exists, Fakka needs a landing page that announces it and collects a waitlist.

**Why it exists**
1. Find out whether people want Fakka.
2. Build a list of early users to invite on launch day.
3. Grow that list through people inviting their friends.

**What a visitor can do**
- Understand in a few seconds what Fakka does and who it is for.
- Read short answers to common questions.
- Join the waitlist with their name and email.
- See their place in the queue, for example "You're #1,234".
- Get a personal invite link. Each friend who joins through it moves them up the queue.
- Receive an email with their place in the queue and their invite link.

**Rules visitors can rely on**
- One person, one place: the same email joins only once, however it is typed.
- Inviting is fair: you cannot invite yourself, and each friend counts once.
- The list is real: automated signups cannot flood it.

**Not part of the launch page:** the expense app itself, accounts or passwords, payments, a public leaderboard, and languages other than Arabic and English.

## Languages

The page speaks Arabic and English.
- A visitor sees the page in their own language first and can switch at any time.
- The email arrives in the language they signed up in.
- Both versions say the same thing, written naturally for each language. Neither is a word-for-word translation.
- The Arabic version reads right to left, like any Arabic page.

## Voice

- **Arabic:** simple, friendly Arabic that reads naturally to Egyptians. No stiff, formal phrasing.
- **English:** short sentences and plain words.
- **Both:** written for someone reading on a phone between two other things. Friendly and honest, no finance jargon, no hype. Amounts are in Egyptian pounds (EGP).
- Never promise a launch date, a price, or a feature that is not in this brief.

## Type

- **Arabic:** Thmanyah (ثمانية). Thmanyah Sans for text, Thmanyah Serif Display for headings.
- **English:** IBM Plex Sans.

## Privacy

We keep only what the waitlist needs: name, email, preferred language, when they joined, and who invited whom. We never share or sell it, and we only email people about Fakka's launch.

## What success looks like

- Most visitors understand what Fakka is without scrolling.
- A meaningful share of visitors join the waitlist.
- People who join bring in friends.
- Every person who joins gets the right place in the queue and their email.
